# 🧠 Portfolio Mission

The technical projects in this portfolio answer questions such as:

> **What happened?**

This folder answers the harder question:

> **What should the product team do about it?**

The objective is to demonstrate the difference between:

**Data Analysis**

and

**Product Analytics Judgment**

The framework is:

**🔍 Observation → 📊 Evidence → 🧠 Interpretation → 🎯 Priority → 🧪 Validation → 💰 Impact → ✅ Decision**

---

# 🏆 Product Decision Framework

![Framework](https://img.shields.io/badge/FRAMEWORK-OBSERVATION_%E2%86%92_DECISION-7C3AED?style=for-the-badge)

Every major insight follows:

### 🔍 Observation

What behavior or business pattern occurred?

### 📊 Evidence

Which metrics support the observation?

### 💼 Why It Matters

Why should Product, Growth, or leadership care?

### 🧠 Root-Cause Hypothesis

What might explain the behavior?

### 💡 Product Recommendation

What should change?

### 🎯 Priority

Why should this opportunity be addressed now?

### 🧪 Experiment

How should the recommendation be validated?

### 📏 Success Metric

What determines whether the intervention worked?

### 🛡️ Guardrail

What must remain healthy?

### 💰 Business Impact

What value could the improvement create?

### ✅ Decision

**Ship • Iterate • Stop • Investigate**

# 🚨 Weak Insight vs Strong Insight

## ❌ Weak

> Mobile conversion is lower.

This is an observation.

It does not tell Product what to do.

---

## ✅ Strong

> Mobile users show materially weaker View → Cart conversion despite high traffic volume, indicating a high-reach product-detail friction opportunity. Prioritize investigation of CTA visibility, page performance, and decision-support content, then validate a mobile PDP treatment using View → Cart conversion as the primary metric and AOV/performance as guardrails.

This connects:

**Observation → Evidence → Hypothesis → Priority → Experiment → Decision**

# 🧠 Root-Cause Analysis

![RCA](https://img.shields.io/badge/ANALYSIS-ROOT_CAUSE_HYPOTHESES-7C3AED?style=for-the-badge)

Product Analytics should distinguish between:

### What the data shows

and

### Why the behavior may be happening

Analytics can often identify **where** the problem occurs.

It may not prove **why**.

Potential root-cause tools include:

* Funnel decomposition
* Segment analysis
* Cohort analysis
* Journey analysis
* Behavioral sequence analysis
* User research
* Session replay
* Support tickets
* Surveys
* Usability testing

### Principle

> **Analytics identifies evidence. Product discovery strengthens the explanation.**

---

# ❓ Five Whys Framework

A simple root-cause structure:

### Why 1

Why did the KPI decline?

### Why 2

Which segment created the decline?

### Why 3

Which behavior changed?

### Why 4

What product experience may influence that behavior?

### Why 5

What underlying customer need or system issue may be responsible?

The goal is not to force exactly five answers.

The goal is to avoid stopping at the surface metric.

# 🧮 RICE Framework

```text
RICE
=
(Reach × Impact × Confidence)
÷
Effort
```

### Reach

How many users are affected?

### Impact

How strongly could the change improve the target outcome?

### Confidence

How trustworthy is the evidence?

### Effort

How expensive is the solution to implement?

### Principle

> **RICE supports judgment. It does not replace judgment.**

---

# 🥇 Priority Levels

## 🔴 P0 — Act / Test Now

High impact, strong evidence, meaningful urgency.

## 🟠 P1 — Next

Important opportunity but lower urgency or confidence.

## 🟡 P2 — Explore

Promising but needs stronger evidence.

## ⚪ Backlog

Low current strategic value.

# 🧪 Experimentation Framework

![Experiment](https://img.shields.io/badge/EXPERIMENTATION-EVIDENCE_%3E_ASSUMPTION-9333EA?style=for-the-badge)

Analytics identifies opportunities.

Experiments determine causality.

The standard experiment workflow is:

**1. Observation**

↓

**2. Hypothesis**

↓

**3. Treatment**

↓

**4. Primary Metric**

↓

**5. Secondary Metrics**

↓

**6. Guardrails**

↓

**7. Sample / Duration**

↓

**8. Analyze**

↓

**9. Decide**

↓

**10. Document**

---

# 🧪 Experiment Hypothesis Template

```text
We believe that
[product change]

for
[target users]

will improve
[target behavior]

because
[evidence / hypothesis].

We will know this is successful when
[primary metric]

improves while
[guardrails]

remain healthy.
```

---

# 📏 Experiment Metric Hierarchy

## 🎯 Primary Metric

The metric the experiment is designed to improve.

## 📊 Secondary Metrics

Additional signals that explain the result.

## 🛡️ Guardrails

Metrics that protect against unintended harm.

## 💰 Business Metric

Connects product improvement to business value.

---

# 🚨 Experiment Decision Rules

Possible outcomes:

### ✅ Ship

Primary metric improves with acceptable guardrails.

### 🔄 Iterate

Evidence is promising but execution or scope should change.

### 🔍 Investigate

Results are ambiguous or conflict across metrics.

### 🛑 Stop

The treatment fails to create meaningful value or damages guardrails.

```text
×
```

```text
×
Cost / Value of Time
```

### Important

> **Impact sizing provides decision context — not guaranteed outcomes.**
